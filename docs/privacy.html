Nexus Calc

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 23 October 2026

Nexus Calc is a calculator app. This page explains, in plain language, what the app does with your information. The short version: there is no account, no advertising, and no tracking. Your calculations stay on your phone.

What stays on your device

  • Everything you type into the calculator, and your calculation history.
  • Your settings — theme, language, number format, keypad layout and the rest.
  • The diagnostics log, which records app events so you can export it when reporting a problem.
  • Usage counters — how often each screen, calculator and tool is opened, and how many AI requests you made. These are simple numbers kept on the device to help diagnose problems and see what is actually used.
  • Crash reports — if the app crashes, the stack trace, your device model and Android version, and the last few log lines are saved so the fault can be found.
  • App-not-responding (ANR) reports — if the app freezes and Android kills it, the system's own record of that exit and its trace are read on the next launch and added to the same local file.
  • Cached exchange rates, so the currency converter works offline.

None of this is uploaded anywhere. The log, the counters and the crash reports are written to this app's private storage and are only shared if you tap Export in Settings and choose where to send them. Uninstalling the app removes all of it.

You can switch collection off, or wipe everything, in Settings → Diagnostics. Collection is on by default so a problem can be diagnosed without you having to reproduce it first.

What is sent off the device

Only when you choose to use these features:

  • Ask AI. Your question, and any photo, audio clip or file you attach, is sent to the app's own backend — a Cloudflare Worker running Cloudflare Workers AI — so a model can answer. The API key is held on that server, not in the app, and you never need to enter one.
  • Voice input. Speech is recognised by your phone's own speech service, governed by your device settings.
  • Currency rates. The app fetches public exchange rates when you open the currency converter with a connection available.
  • Update checks. The app asks GitHub for the latest release version number.

Nothing is attached to your identity. There is no sign-in, no user ID and no profile, so requests cannot be tied back to you.

Optional encrypted sync

Sync is off until you turn it on. If you do:

  • You choose a sync code. It stays on your device and is never sent anywhere.
  • Your own calculators, your settings and (only if you tick the box) your calculation history are encrypted on the device with a key derived from that code, using AES-256-GCM with PBKDF2.
  • What reaches the server is an unreadable blob filed under the SHA-256 hash of your code. The server cannot read your data and cannot work out your code from what it holds.
  • Anyone who knows the code can open the stored copy, so keep it safe. There is no reset — if you lose the code, the stored copy can never be read again. You can delete it at any time from Settings.

What we do not do

  • No advertising and no ad SDKs.
  • No third-party analytics or crash-reporting services — nothing is sent to any such service. The counters and crash reports described above are kept on your device and nowhere else.
  • No selling or sharing of data with third parties.
  • No access to your contacts, location, camera roll or files beyond the specific item you pick to attach.

Permissions the app asks for

  • INTERNET — for Ask AI, currency rates and update checks.
  • REQUEST_INSTALL_PACKAGES — so the in-app updater can hand a downloaded update to Android's installer. Android still asks you to confirm.

The app does not request the camera or microphone permission. When you take a photo or dictate a calculation, Android's own camera and speech apps do the work and hand the result back.

Children

The app is a general-purpose calculator and is not directed at children. It collects no personal information from anyone.
